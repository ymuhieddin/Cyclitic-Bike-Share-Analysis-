# Cyclistic Bike-Share Analysis

**Data Analytics Portfolio Project | January–May 2025**

**Author:** Yazan Muhieddin  
**Tools:** Google BigQuery (SQL), Google Sheets, Tableau Public  
**Dataset:** 1,430,076 cleaned bike-share trips

## Project Overview

This project analyzes bike-share usage patterns to understand how casual riders and annual members use Cyclistic bikes differently.

Using publicly available Divvy bike-share data from January through May 2025, I cleaned and analyzed over 1.43 million trips to identify trends in ride duration, weekday activity, monthly ridership, and hourly usage.

The goal was to develop data-driven marketing recommendations that could help Cyclistic convert casual riders into annual members.

## Business Question

**How do annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently?**

## Tools and Skills

- **SQL (Google BigQuery):** Data cleaning, validation, aggregation, and analysis
- **Google Sheets:** Pivot tables and exploratory visualizations
- **Tableau Public:** Interactive dashboard and data storytelling
- **Business Analytics:** Identifying customer behavior patterns and developing recommendations

## Key Findings

### 1. Casual Riders Take Longer Trips

- Casual riders averaged **22.25 minutes** per ride.
- Annual members averaged **11.44 minutes** per ride.
- Casual rides were approximately **1.94 times longer** on average.

### 2. Annual Members Show Stronger Weekday Activity

- Annual members accounted for approximately **71% of recorded rides**.
- Member usage was strongest during weekdays and typical commuting hours.
- Casual riders showed relatively greater weekend activity, particularly on Saturdays.

### 3. Ridership Increased from January to May

- Casual rides increased from **23,453 in January** to **176,002 in May**.
- Member rides increased from **112,352 in January** to **314,046 in May**.
- Both groups experienced substantial increases in riding activity during the analysis period.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, I recommend:

1. **Target weekend riders:** Test membership promotions designed for casual riders who use bicycles for recreational activities.
2. **Develop spring marketing campaigns:** Test membership promotions ahead of the observed increase in ridership between March and May.
3. **Personalize membership messaging:** Develop different campaigns for recreational riders and customers interested in routine transportation.

These recommendations should be evaluated through controlled marketing experiments to determine their effectiveness.
